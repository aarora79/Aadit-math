1 · Ratios as equal groups

R1One group, then count the groups

A ratio like 2 : 3 compares two amounts. Read it as one small group: 2 of the first kind and 3 of the second, so 5 in each group. Find how many groups fit in the total, then multiply each part.

boys : girls = 2 : 3, and there are 30 students one group: 2 + 3 = 5 students groups: 30 ÷ 5 = 6 boys = 2 × 6 = 12 girls = 3 × 6 = 18 (12 + 18 = 30)
“3 out of every 5” is a part compared with the whole group. The other part is 5 − 3 = 2 out of every 5.

Check yourself: a bag has red and blue marbles in the ratio 4 : 3. There are 28 marbles. How many are blue?

Answer:

one group: 4 + 3 = 7, groups: 28 ÷ 7 = 4 blue = 3 × 4 = 12

2 · Remainders repeat in cycles

R2The pattern starts over at the LCM

The remainder is what is left over after dividing. For example 14 ÷ 3 = 4 remainder 2. As you count up, remainders repeat in a cycle: dividing by 3 gives 1, 2, 0, 1, 2, 0, and so on.

n123456789101112
n ÷ 3 leaves120120120120
n ÷ 4 leaves123012301230

Both rows start over together at 12. That is the least common multiple (LCM) of 3 and 4: the smallest number that is a multiple of both.

To find the LCM, list multiples of the bigger number until one also divides by the smaller. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. 12 divides by 3, so the LCM is 12.

Check yourself: which numbers from 1 to 20 leave remainder 1 when divided by 2 and also remainder 1 when divided by 3?

Answer:

The LCM of 2 and 3 is 6, so the matches repeat every 6, starting at 1.

1, 7, 13, 19 → 4 numbers

3 · Distance, speed and time

R3distance = speed × time

At a constant speed, distance = speed × time. Divide to get the other two: time = distance ÷ speed, and speed = distance ÷ time.

120 km at 40 km per hour time = 120 ÷ 40 = 3 hours

“2 meters per second faster” means add 2 to the speed. A faster runner takes less time over the same distance.

Keep the units matched. Meters with seconds, or kilometers with hours.

Check yourself: one runner covers 60 m at 4 m per second, and another covers the same 60 m at 5 m per second. How many seconds sooner does the faster runner finish?

Answer:

slower: 60 ÷ 4 = 15 seconds faster: 60 ÷ 5 = 12 seconds 15 − 12 = 3 seconds sooner

4 · Clearing fractions and factoring

R4Multiply by the bottoms, then factor

When an equation has x on the bottom of fractions, multiply every term by all the bottoms. The fractions disappear. If you get an x², move everything to one side and factor.

12/x − 12/(x + 2) = 1 multiply by x(x + 2): 12(x + 2) − 12x = x(x + 2) 24 = x² + 2x one side: x² + 2x − 24 = 0

To factor x² + bx + c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. Here they multiply to −24 and add to 2: that is 6 and −4.

(x + 6)(x − 4) = 0 → x = −6 or x = 4
If two things multiply to 0, one of them is 0. A time or a speed cannot be negative, so here x = 4.

Check yourself: solve x² + x − 12 = 0 for positive x.

Answer:

Two numbers that multiply to −12 and add to 1 are 4 and −3.

(x + 4)(x − 3) = 0 → x = 3 (x = −4 is not positive)

5 · Mean and median

R5Total and middle

The mean is the total divided by how many numbers there are. Turn it around: total = mean × count.

The median is the middle number once the list is in order. With an even count there are two middle numbers, and the median is halfway between them. So the two middle numbers add to 2 × median.

4, 7, 9, 12 mean = (4 + 7 + 9 + 12) ÷ 4 = 32 ÷ 4 = 8 median = (7 + 9) ÷ 2 = 8
Always put the numbers in order first. The median depends on the order, the mean does not.

Check yourself: five numbers have a mean of 10. What is their total? What is the median of 3, 8, 10, 15?

Answer:

total = 10 × 5 = 50 median = (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9

6 · Adding 1 + 2 + 3 + … + n

R6Two staircases make a rectangle

Write the sum forward and backward. Each column adds to n + 1, and there are n columns. That is twice the sum, so halve it.

1 + 2 + 3 + 4 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 4 × 5 = 20, so the sum is 10 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n + 1) ÷ 2

These sums 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, … are called triangular numbers. To find the biggest n with a sum under a limit, note that n(n + 1) is close to n². So start with n near the square root of 2 × limit, then test.

Check the number just above your guess too. The answer is the last n whose sum still fits.

Check yourself: what is 1 + 2 + … + 20? What is the greatest n with 1 + 2 + … + n ≤ 100?

Answer:

20 × 21 ÷ 2 = 210 2 × 100 = 200, and 14 × 14 = 196, so try n = 13 and 14: 13 × 14 ÷ 2 = 91 fits 14 × 15 ÷ 2 = 105 too big n = 13

7 · Areas of rectangles that share a side

R7Same height, areas follow the widths

The area of a rectangle is width × height. When two straight cuts split a rectangle into four pieces, side-by-side pieces share a height and stacked pieces share a width. If two pieces share a height, their areas are in the same ratio as their widths.

Widths 3 and 1 are marked on the top and bottom, heights 2 and 1 on both sides. The shaded piece has area 6 out of a total of 12.

top row: 6 and 2, widths 3 and 1, so 6 is 3 times 2 whole: 4 × 3 = 12 = 6 + 2 + 3 + 1
Give the unknown widths and heights letters, like w and h. Areas then become products like 3w × h, and the letters often cancel.

Check yourself: two rectangles sit side by side with the same height. The left one has area 24 and width 6. The right one has area 8. What is the right one's width?

Answer:

height = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 width = 8 ÷ 4 = 2

Or use the ratio: 24 is 3 times 8, so the left width is 3 times the right width. 6 ÷ 3 = 2.

8 · Probability as a fraction of the area

R8Part of the area over the whole area

When a dart is equally likely to land anywhere on a board, the probability of landing in a region is region area ÷ total area. Write it as a fraction and simplify.

in the figure in section 7: P(shaded) = 6 / 12 = 1/2

It works backward too: if you know the probability and the total, then region area = probability × total area.

To simplify a fraction, divide the top and bottom by the same number until nothing else divides both.

Check yourself: a board has area 40. One region has area 6. What is the probability that a dart lands in that region?

Answer:

6/40 = 3/20 (divide top and bottom by 2)
