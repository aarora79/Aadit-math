Problem 2 · Remainders

James's number between 10 and 30

The question James is thinking of a whole number between 10 and 30, inclusive. James tells Selena the remainder when his number is divided by 3, and also the remainder when his number is divided by 4, and Selena observes that these remainders are different. How many possible numbers could James be thinking of?
02Remainders run in cycles

A remainder counts up, then starts over. By 3 it starts over every 3 numbers. By 4 it takes 4. They line up again every 12 numbers, the least common multiple of 3 and 4.

The stripes match only at the start of each block. Count those cases. Then check whether the question wants them or wants all the rest.

Step by step

  1. Find when the two remainders can even be equal

    A remainder after dividing by 3 is 0, 1 or 2. A remainder after dividing by 4 is 0, 1, 2 or 3. For the two to match, both have to be 0, 1 or 2.

  2. Find where the cycles line up

    Both counts restart together at every multiple of 12.

    at a multiple of 12 → both remainders 0 one more than that → both 1 two more than that → both 2 three more than that → by 3 gives 0, by 4 gives 3

    From three past the multiple onward, the two never agree again until the next multiple of 12.

  3. List the matching numbers between 10 and 30

    The multiples of 12 in range are 12 and 24. Take each one plus 0, 1 and 2.

    12, 13, 14, 24, 25, 26 → 6 numbers

  4. Count all the numbers in the range

    Inclusive, so add 1 after subtracting.

    30 − 10 + 1 = 21 numbers

  5. Subtract, because the question wants the ones that differ

    21 − 6 = 15
