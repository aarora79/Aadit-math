Problem 1 · Super easy · Counting primes

Primes from 20 to 40

The question How many prime numbers are there between 20 and 40?
Hint 1
01A prime has exactly two factors

A prime has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. To test a number, divide it by the small primes 2, 3, 5, 7, and so on. Stop when the prime times itself is bigger than the number.

Is 37 prime? 37 ÷ 2 no 37 ÷ 3 no 37 ÷ 5 no 7 × 7 = 49 is bigger than 37, so stop. 37 is prime.
1 is not prime: it has only one factor. 2 is the only even prime.
Hint 2

Every number from 21 to 39 is less than 7 × 7 = 49. So you only need to test 2, 3 and 5. Cross out the multiples of each one.

Step-by-step solution

Step by step

  1. List the numbers

    “Between 20 and 40” means 21 up to 39.

  2. Cross out the even numbers

    Even numbers divide by 2, so they are not prime. That leaves the odd numbers.

    21 23 25 27 29 31 33 35 37 39

  3. Cross out multiples of 3

    A number divides by 3 when its digits add up to a multiple of 3. 21, 27, 33 and 39 all do (2+1 = 3, 2+7 = 9, 3+3 = 6, 3+9 = 12).

    23 25 29 31 35 37

  4. Cross out multiples of 5

    Numbers ending in 5 divide by 5. That removes 25 and 35.

    23 29 31 37

  5. Why you can stop

    The next prime is 7, and 7 × 7 = 49 is bigger than 39. A number under 49 that is not prime must have a factor of 2, 3 or 5, and those are all crossed out already.

  6. Count

    23, 29, 31, 37 → 4 primes
There are 4 primes: 23, 29, 31 and 37.

How to spot this type: “how many primes” in a range. Cross out multiples of 2, then 3, then 5, and keep going only while the prime squared is not bigger than the top of the range. Do not count 1 as prime.
