Problem 2 · Easy · Prime factors

The primes inside 990

The question What is the sum of the distinct prime factors of 990?
Hint 1
02Break a number into primes

Divide by the smallest prime that goes in. Repeat with what is left until you reach 1. The primes you divided by are the prime factors. Every whole number above 1 breaks into primes in only one way.

2 | 990 3 | 495 3 | 165 5 | 55 11 | 11 | 1
“Distinct” means different. If a prime shows up twice, count it only once.
Hint 2

990 is even, so start by dividing by 2. Keep going until you reach 1. The prime 3 shows up twice, but you add it only once.

Step-by-step solution

Step by step

  1. Divide by 2

    990 is even.

    990 ÷ 2 = 495

  2. Divide by 3, twice

    The digits of 495 add to 4 + 9 + 5 = 18, a multiple of 3. The digits of 165 add to 12, so 3 goes in again.

    495 ÷ 3 = 165 165 ÷ 3 = 55

  3. Divide by 5, then 11

    55 ends in 5. What is left, 11, is prime.

    55 ÷ 5 = 11 11 ÷ 11 = 1

  4. Write the factorization

    Check it by multiplying back.

    990 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 × 11 2 × 9 × 5 × 11 = 990, so it checks out

  5. Add the different primes

    The different primes are 2, 3, 5 and 11. Count 3 only once.

    2 + 3 + 5 + 11 = 21
21

How to spot this type: “distinct prime factors”. Factor completely, then list each prime once. If you add 3 twice you get 24, which is the trap answer.
