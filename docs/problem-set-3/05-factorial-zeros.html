Problem 5 · Medium · Factorial zeros

The zeros at the end of 50!

The question The number 50! (said “50 factorial”) means 50 × 49 × 48 × … × 2 × 1. When 50! is written out, how many zeros are at the end?
Hint 1
05Each end zero is a 2 times a 5

Each zero at the end comes from a factor of 10, and 10 = 2 × 5. A factorial has many more 2s than 5s, so count the 5s. A multiple of 25 holds two 5s, so it counts twice.

10! = 3628800 (2 zeros) fives come from 5 and 10 → 2 fives
Count multiples of 5, then multiples of 25, then 125, and add them up.
Hint 2

How many numbers from 1 to 50 are multiples of 5? Two of them, 25 and 50, each hold an extra 5.

Step-by-step solution

Step by step

  1. Where an end zero comes from

    Each zero at the end means one more factor of 10, and 10 = 2 × 5.

  2. Count only the 5s

    Every even number gives a 2, so there are far more 2s than 5s. Each 5 can find a 2 to pair with. The number of zeros is the number of 5s.

  3. Multiples of 5

    Each of these gives one 5.

    5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50 50 ÷ 5 = 10

  4. Multiples of 25 give an extra 5

    25 = 5 × 5 and 50 = 2 × 5 × 5. Each holds a second 5 that step 3 did not count.

    50 ÷ 25 = 2

  5. Add

    125 is bigger than 50, so there are no more.

    10 + 2 = 12
50! ends in 12 zeros.

How to spot this type: “zeros at the end” of a factorial. Divide by 5, 25, 125, and so on, drop the remainders, and add. Forgetting the 25s gives 10, a common wrong answer.
