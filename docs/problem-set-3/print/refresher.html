1 · What a prime is

R1Exactly two factors

A factor of a number divides it with no remainder. A prime is a whole number with exactly two factors: 1 and itself. A whole number above 1 that is not prime is called composite.

7: factors 1, 7 → prime 6: factors 1, 2, 3, 6 → composite 1: factor 1 only → neither

1 is not prime, because it has only one factor. 2 is the only even prime: every other even number divides by 2.

Check yourself: which of these are prime: 1, 2, 9, 17, 21, 29?

Answer:

2, 17 and 29. The number 1 has only one factor, 9 = 3 × 3 and 21 = 3 × 7.

2 · Testing a number for prime

R2Divide by small primes, stop at the square

To test a number, divide it by the primes 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and so on. Stop when the prime times itself is bigger than the number. If nothing divided it, the number is prime.

Why you can stop: if a number is a × b, the smaller of a and b is at most the square root. So a factor always shows up early.

Know your squares: 7 × 7 = 49, 11 × 11 = 121, 13 × 13 = 169. For a number under 121 you only need to test 2, 3, 5 and 7.

Check yourself: is 97 prime?

Answer:

Yes. 10 × 10 = 100 is bigger than 97, so test only 2, 3, 5 and 7. 97 is odd, its digits add to 16, it ends in 7, and 7 × 13 = 91 while 7 × 14 = 98. Nothing divides it, so 97 is prime.

3 · Breaking a number into primes

R3The prime factorization

Divide by the smallest prime that goes in, then keep going with what is left until you reach 1. Every whole number above 1 breaks into primes in only one way. This is its prime factorization.

2 | 60 2 | 30 3 | 15 5 | 5 | 1 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 2² × 3 × 5

A small number written up high is a power. It counts how many times the prime is used: 2² means 2 × 2 and 25 means 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2. A prime with no power written has power 1.

Check a factorization by multiplying it back. 4 × 3 × 5 = 60.

Check yourself: write 84 as a product of primes, using powers.

Answer:

84 ÷ 2 = 42, 42 ÷ 2 = 21, 21 ÷ 3 = 7, 7 ÷ 7 = 1 84 = 2² × 3 × 7

4 · Odd and even

R4Odd plus odd is even

An even number is a multiple of 2. An odd number is one more than a multiple of 2. When you add, the result follows a fixed pattern.

addresultexample
odd + oddeven3 + 5 = 8
odd + evenodd3 + 4 = 7
even + eveneven4 + 6 = 10
Every prime except 2 is odd. So two primes can only add up to an odd number if one of them is 2.

Check yourself: can 35 be written as the sum of two primes?

Answer:

No. 35 is odd, so one of the primes would have to be 2, and the other would be 35 − 2 = 33. But 33 = 3 × 11 is not prime.

5 · Counting divisors

R5Choices for each power

Divisor is another word for factor. Every divisor of a number is built from the same primes, using each one from zero times up to its power. Count the choices for each prime and multiply.

12 = 2² × 3 power of 2: 0, 1 or 2 (3 choices) power of 3: 0 or 1 (2 choices) 3 × 2 = 6 divisors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

The short rule: add 1 to each power, then multiply.

Using a prime zero times is a real choice. That is how the divisor 1 gets counted.

Check yourself: how many divisors does 18 have?

Answer:

18 = 2 × 3² (1 + 1) × (2 + 1) = 2 × 3 = 6 divisors: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18

6 · Factorials and the primes inside them

R6Count the multiples

The factorial n! (said “n factorial”) multiplies every whole number from n down to 1. For example 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120.

To count how many times a prime divides n!, count its multiples up to n. Then count the multiples of its square, which give a second copy, then its cube, and so on. Drop any remainders.

How many 2s are in 10!? multiples of 2: 10 ÷ 2 = 5 (2, 4, 6, 8, 10) multiples of 4: 10 ÷ 4 = 2 (4, 8) multiples of 8: 10 ÷ 8 = 1 (8) total: 5 + 2 + 1 = 8, so 10! = 28 × (odd number)
A zero at the end of a number is a factor of 10 = 2 × 5. In a factorial the 5s run out first, so count the 5s.

Check yourself: how many times does 3 divide 20!?

Answer:

multiples of 3: 20 ÷ 3 = 6 (3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18) multiples of 9: 20 ÷ 9 = 2 (9, 18) 27 is bigger than 20, so stop. 6 + 2 = 8

7 · Remainders when you divide by 3

R7Every number is 3k, 3k + 1 or 3k + 2

Divide any whole number by 3 and the remainder is 0, 1 or 2. So every whole number can be written as 3k, 3k + 1 or 3k + 2 for some whole number k. Remainder 2 is the same as one less than a multiple of 3, so 3k + 2 can also be written 3k − 1.

12 = 3 × 4 remainder 0 13 = 3 × 4 + 1 remainder 1 14 = 3 × 5 − 1 remainder 2

A prime above 3 is never a multiple of 3, so it is always 3k + 1 or 3k − 1.

Algebra helps here: (3k + 1)² = 9k² + 6k + 1. The first two parts are multiples of 3, so the square leaves remainder 1.

Check yourself: what remainder does 23 leave when divided by 3? What remainder does 23² = 529 leave?

Answer:

23 = 3 × 7 + 2 remainder 2 (also 23 = 3 × 8 − 1) 529 = 3 × 176 + 1 remainder 1

8 · Perfect squares

R8Squares have only even powers

A perfect square is a whole number times itself, like 36 = 6 × 6. When you square a number, every power in its prime factorization doubles. So in a perfect square every power is even.

6 = 2 × 3 → 36 = 2² × 3² even powers, square 200 = 2³ × 5² 2³ is odd, not a square

So a square divisor of a number uses an even power, 0, 2, 4, and so on, for every prime.

Zero counts as even. Using a prime zero times is allowed.

Check yourself: 144 = 24 × 3². How many of its divisors are perfect squares?

Answer:

power of 2: 0, 2 or 4 (3 choices) power of 3: 0 or 2 (2 choices) 3 × 2 = 6 square divisors: 1, 4, 9, 16, 36, 144
