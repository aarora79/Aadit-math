01A prime has exactly two factors

A prime has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. To test a number, divide it by the small primes 2, 3, 5, 7, and so on. Stop when the prime times itself is bigger than the number.

Is 37 prime? 37 ÷ 2 no 37 ÷ 3 no 37 ÷ 5 no 7 × 7 = 49 is bigger than 37, so stop. 37 is prime.
1 is not prime: it has only one factor. 2 is the only even prime.

02Break a number into primes

Divide by the smallest prime that goes in. Repeat with what is left until you reach 1. The primes you divided by are the prime factors. Every whole number above 1 breaks into primes in only one way.

2 | 990 3 | 495 3 | 165 5 | 55 11 | 11 | 1
“Distinct” means different. If a prime shows up twice, count it only once.

05Each end zero is a 2 times a 5

Each zero at the end comes from a factor of 10, and 10 = 2 × 5. A factorial has many more 2s than 5s, so count the 5s. A multiple of 25 holds two 5s, so it counts twice.

07Every prime above 3 sits next to a multiple of 3

08A perfect square uses only even powers

In a perfect square every prime appears an even number of times. So a square divisor picks an even power, 0, 2, 4, and so on, for each prime, up to that prime's power in the number. To find the powers inside a factorial, count multiples, as with the end zeros.

72 = 2³ × 3² power of 2: 0 or 2 (2 choices) power of 3: 0 or 2 (2 choices) 2 × 2 = 4 square divisors: 1, 4, 9, 36
If a prime has power e, the even choices are 0, 2, 4, … up to e. That is (e ÷ 2, rounded down) + 1 choices.
