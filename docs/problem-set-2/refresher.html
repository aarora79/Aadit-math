1 · Turning words into algebra

R1One letter, then translate each phrase

Pick one letter for one unknown, usually the smaller one. Write every other amount using that same letter. Then turn the sentence into an equation, one phrase at a time.

“Years ago” and “years from now” change both people's ages by the same amount.

Check yourself: Maya is 3 times as old as Theo. In 4 years, Maya will be twice as old as Theo. How old is Theo now?

Show the answer
3t + 4 = 2(t + 4) 3t + 4 = 2t + 8 t = 4

Check: Maya is 12. In 4 years she is 16 and Theo is 8, and 16 is twice 8.

2 · Solving x² equations by factoring

R2Multiply out, collect, factor

To square a bracket, multiply it by itself: (x − 3)² = (x − 3)(x − 3) = x² − 6x + 9. Then move every term to one side so the other side is 0.

To factor x² + bx + c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.

x² − 7x + 10 = 0 multiply to 10, add to −7: −2 and −5 (x − 2)(x − 5) = 0 x = 2 or x = 5
An x² equation usually has two answers. Use the extra facts in the problem, like “older than 8”, to throw one away.

Check yourself: solve x² − 9x + 18 = 0 when x is bigger than 4.

Show the answer

Two numbers that multiply to 18 and add to −9 are −3 and −6.

(x − 3)(x − 6) = 0 → x = 3 or x = 6 x is bigger than 4, so x = 6

3 · Angles in a triangle

R3The three angles add to 180°

In every triangle, the three angles add up to 180°. If you know two angles, subtract them from 180° to get the third.

The two known angles are 50° and 60°. The third angle is unknown. The letters a, b and c name the sides.

180° − 50° − 60° = 70°.

If two angles add to 90°, the third is 180° − 90° = 90°. That is a right angle, so the triangle is a right triangle.

Check yourself: a triangle has angles of 30° and 45°. What is the third angle?

Show the answer
180° − 30° − 45° = 105°

4 · Special right triangles

R4Two triangles with fixed side patterns

A right triangle has one 90° angle. Its longest side, across from the right angle, is the hypotenuse. The other two sides are the legs. Two right triangles come up again and again, and their sides always follow the same pattern.

45-45-90: the two legs are equal. The hypotenuse is a leg times √2.

30-60-90: the short leg x is across from 30°. The hypotenuse is 2x and the long leg is x√3.

30-60-90 with hypotenuse 10: short leg = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 long leg = 5√3 ≈ 5 × 1.732 = 8.66 45-45-90 with hypotenuse 10: each leg = 10 ÷ √2 = 5√2 ≈ 7.07
Remember √2 ≈ 1.414 and √3 ≈ 1.732. In a 30-60-90 triangle the short leg is always across from the 30° angle.

Check yourself: a 45-45-90 triangle has legs of 6. How long is the hypotenuse? A 30-60-90 triangle has a short leg of 4. How long are the other two sides?

Show the answer
45-45-90: hypotenuse = 6√2 ≈ 8.49 30-60-90: long leg = 4√3 ≈ 6.93, hypotenuse = 2 × 4 = 8

5 · Area of a triangle

R5Half of base times height

The area of a triangle is ½ × base × height. The height must meet the base at a right angle. In a right triangle the two legs already do, so the area is ½ × leg × leg.

right triangle with legs 6 and 8 area = ½ × 6 × 8 = 24

To find the area of a shape made of two triangles, find each triangle's area and add them. The figure in section 7 shows this triangle.

Do not use the hypotenuse as the height. It does not meet the base at a right angle.

Check yourself: a right triangle has legs 5 and 12. What is its area?

Show the answer
½ × 5 × 12 = 30

6 · Price per piece and units

R6Divide the total by the count

The price per piece (a unit price) is the total cost divided by the number of pieces. Before dividing, change the money into the unit the answer asks for. $1 = 100 cents.

$6 for 15 pencils $6 = 600 cents 600 ÷ 15 = 40 cents per pencil
Only divide by the pieces that were paid for. To compare two people, find each one's price per piece first, then subtract.

Check yourself: 9 apples cost $4.50. How many cents is that per apple?

Show the answer
$4.50 = 450 cents 450 ÷ 9 = 50 cents per apple

7 · The Pythagorean theorem

R7leg² + leg² = hypotenuse²

In a right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c, a² + b² = c². Use it to find any side from the other two.

The legs are 6 and 8. The hypotenuse, across from the right angle, is unknown.

6² + 8² = c² 36 + 64 = c² 100 = c² → c = 10

The hypotenuse is 10. The legs are the base and the height, so the area is ½ × 8 × 6 = 24.

Some sets of whole numbers fit exactly: 3, 4, 5 and 5, 12, 13 and 8, 15, 17, plus their multiples, like 6, 8, 10.

Check yourself: a right triangle has one leg 5 and hypotenuse 13. How long is the other leg?

Show the answer
5² + b² = 13² 25 + b² = 169 b² = 144 → b = 12

8 · Similar shapes and area

R8Area grows with the side squared

Shapes are similar when one is a scaled copy of the other. All squares are similar, and so are all equilateral triangles and all regular pentagons. If every side is multiplied by k, the area is multiplied by k².

Doubling the side from 2 to 4 makes the area 4 times bigger: 4 becomes 16.

So for similar shapes, area = (a fixed number) × side². If a price is based on area, the price follows side² too.

Similar shapes built on the three sides of a right triangle follow Pythagoras: the two small areas add up to the big one.

Check yourself: every side of a triangle is tripled. How many times bigger is its area? Two similar shapes have areas in the ratio 25 : 9. What is the ratio of their sides?

Show the answer
sides × 3 → area × 3² = 9 area 25 : 9 → sides √25 : √9 = 5 : 3
