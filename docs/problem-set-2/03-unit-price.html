Problem 3 · Unit price

Matthew's candy

The question Matthew buys a bag of 50 pieces of candy for $8. He eats 25 pieces and sells the remaining 25 pieces to Darren for $5. How many more cents per piece does Darren pay, on average, than Matthew?
03Price per piece is cost divided by count

Change dollars to cents first, then divide by the number of pieces each person paid for. Now the two prices are in the same unit and you can subtract.

Matthew16¢
Darren20¢
Ignore what nobody paid for. The 25 pieces Matthew ate do not change what each piece cost him.

Step by step

  1. Change the money to cents

    The answer is asked in cents, so work in cents from the start.

    $8 = 800 cents $5 = 500 cents

  2. Matthew's price per piece

    Matthew paid for the whole bag of 50.

    800 ÷ 50 = 16 cents per piece

  3. Darren's price per piece

    Darren paid for 25 pieces.

    500 ÷ 25 = 20 cents per piece

  4. Subtract

    20 − 16 = 4 cents

  5. Check the units

    The question asks for “how many more cents per piece”. That is a difference of two per-piece prices, which is 4, not a difference of totals. If you subtract the totals you get 800 − 500 = 300 cents, which answers a different question.

Darren pays 4 more cents per piece.

How to spot this type: “per piece”, “each” or “on average” next to a money total. Divide each total by its own count, in the unit the answer wants, and only then compare. Extra story details, like the candy Matthew ate, are often there to distract you.
