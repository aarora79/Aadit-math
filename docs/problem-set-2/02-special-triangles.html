Problem 2 · Special triangles

The quadrilateral with a 26 cm diagonal

The question What is the area of the quadrilateral shown, with diagonal 26 cm? Express your answer as a decimal to the nearest hundredth.

The figure: a dashed diagonal of 26 cm runs from the bottom-left corner to the top-right corner. At the bottom-left corner the diagonal makes 30° with the left side and 45° with the bottom side. At the top-right corner it makes 60° with the top side and 45° with the right side.

Hint 1

02A diagonal cuts out special triangles

Add up the angles at each end of the diagonal. If a triangle's two known angles make 90°, its third angle is a right angle and the diagonal is the hypotenuse.

30-60-90 sides go 1 : √3 : 2. 45-45-90 sides go 1 : 1 : √2. Find each triangle's area on its own, then add.
Hint 2 Add the two angles in each triangle: 30 + 60 and 45 + 45. Both make 90, so each triangle has a right angle, and the 26 cm diagonal is the hypotenuse of both.

Step by step

  1. Split the shape along the diagonal

    The diagonal cuts the quadrilateral into an upper triangle and a lower triangle. Both have the 26 cm diagonal as one side.

  2. Find the third angle of each triangle

    upper: 180 − 30 − 60 = 90 lower: 180 − 45 − 45 = 90

    Both are right triangles, and in both the diagonal sits opposite the right angle. So 26 is the hypotenuse each time.

  3. Upper triangle: 30-60-90

    The short leg is half the hypotenuse. The long leg is the short leg times √3.

    short leg = 26 ÷ 2 = 13 long leg = 13√3 area = ½ × 13 × 13√3 = 84.5√3 ≈ 146.36

  4. Lower triangle: 45-45-90

    Both legs are equal, and each leg times √2 gives the hypotenuse.

    leg = 26 ÷ √2 area = ½ × leg × leg = ½ × 676 ÷ 2 = 169

    Shortcut: the area of a 45-45-90 triangle is hypotenuse² ÷ 4.

  5. Add the two areas

    169 + 84.5√3 = 169 + 84.5 × 1.7320508… = 169 + 146.358… = 315.358… → 315.36

  6. Sanity check

    A square with diagonal 26 has area 26² ÷ 2 = 338. A square has the biggest possible area for that diagonal when both triangles are 45-45-90. Our upper triangle is a little thinner, so the answer should come out a little under 338. It does.

315.36 cm²

How to spot this type: a shape with one diagonal drawn and angles marked at its ends. Add the angles in each triangle. When they reach 90° you have a right triangle, and when the angles are 30, 45 or 60 the side ratios are already known. Keep √3 in the answer until the last step, then round once.
