Problem 4 · Similar areas

Pentagonal cookies

The question A geometric baker makes pentagonal cookies, as shown below. Cookies are priced in proportion to their area, and two cookies have prices as indicated. What is the area of the right triangle shown?

The 12 in. leg is the side shared with the $9 cookie. The hypotenuse is the side shared with the $21.25 cookie.

Hint 1

04Area grows with the side squared

Shapes with the same form are similar. Double the side and the area goes up 4 times. So area, and a price based on area, is a fixed number times side².

Similar shapes on the three sides of a right triangle obey Pythagoras too: the two small ones add up to the big one. Use the prices as areas.
Hint 2 Write price = k × side². The $9 cookie has side 12, so 9 = 144k. Use that k to get the hypotenuse squared from $21.25, then Pythagoras gives the other leg.

Step by step

  1. Read the picture

    Each pentagon shares one whole side with the triangle. Call the unknown leg a and the hypotenuse c.

    $9 cookie → side 12 (a leg) $21.25 cookie → side c (the hypotenuse) no price → side a (the other leg)

  2. Price works like side squared

    All regular pentagons are similar, so a pentagon's area is some fixed number k times side². The price is proportional to the area, so price = k × side² for one fixed k.

    9 = k × 12² = 144k → k = 9/144 = 1/16

  3. Find the hypotenuse squared

    21.25 = c² ÷ 16 → c² = 21.25 × 16 = 340

  4. Pythagoras gives the other leg

    a² + 12² = c² a² + 144 = 340 a² = 196 → a = 14

    Faster: the cookies follow Pythagoras too. The missing cookie costs 21.25 − 9 = $12.25, and 12.25 × 16 = 196 = 14².

  5. Area of the right triangle

    The two legs are the base and the height.

    ½ × 12 × 14 = 84

  6. Watch the trap

    340 is c², the hypotenuse squared. It is not an area of anything in the question. Keep going until you have the triangle itself.

84 in²

How to spot this type: the same shape drawn at different sizes, with a price, weight or area given for some of them. Write everything as k × side², find k from the one you know fully, and the rest follow. When the shapes sit on the sides of a right triangle, Pythagoras adds up their areas as well as the squares.
